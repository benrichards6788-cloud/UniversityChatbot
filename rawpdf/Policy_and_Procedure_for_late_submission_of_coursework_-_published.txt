Policy and procedure for late submission of coursework 
Rationale 
1. This policy aims to ensure an institution-wide approach to handling the late submission of students’ 
coursework.  
2. The policy is underpinned by a commitment to the fundamental objectives of fairness and of 
supporting students in their studies while recognising the respective responsibilities of students and 
staff.  
3. This policy must be implemented in line with the following other related policies: Assessment and 
Feedback Policy, the Policy on Extensions to the Submission of Coursework, the Personal 
Circumstances and Academic Appeals Procedure, and programme regulations.  
  
Definitions  
4. Coursework is defined broadly as a piece of work created by students during their programme of 
study, that is assessed and counts towards a final mark or grade in a module. 
5. Coursework is deemed to be late when it is submitted after the published submission deadline 
without an agreed extension.  
6. Published submission dates and times shall be set to UK local time and it is the responsibility of 
students outside this time zone to check the corresponding submission date and time in their 
locale. 
  
Scope  
7. Except where not permitted for reasons of professional accreditation, the penalties for late 
submission of coursework shall apply to all years of undergraduate and postgraduate taught 
programmes.  
8. In the event of a professional body indicating concern about the implementation of this policy within 
accredited programmes, Department staff should refer the issue to the relevant Vice Dean 
(Academic).  
9. For some forms of continuous or integrated assessments such as projects and dissertations, the 
application of penalties for late submission may not be appropriate or feasible. In these cases, 
Departments are authorised to introduce appropriate variations to the penalty scheme set out in 
this policy. Any variation to the scheme must be communicated to students clearly in advance, and 
must be fair and supportive of students in their studies.

10. For some forms of assessment including examinations, class tests, presentations, vivas, assessed 
observations and practicals, where the application of late penalties is not appropriate, this policy 
will not apply. 
  
Principles and guidelines for implementation  
11. All staff should adhere to the following guiding principles and procedures in handling coursework 
that is submitted late as defined above in paragraph 5.  
12. The application of the policy should not adversely impact student progression where coursework 
has been submitted up to and including 7 calendar days late and is of a pass standard.  
 
Coursework submission deadline  
13. It is recommended that, where feasible and the structure of the programme allows, deadlines for 
the submission of coursework should be set between 12.00 and 18.00 during the University’s 
standard working week, and preferably between Monday and Thursday. This is so that students 
can access University support services if required close to the submission deadline. In addition, 
any essential technical developments to Myplace are normally scheduled to take place outside of 
these times.  
14. Penalties will be applied to late submitted coursework up to and including seven calendar days 
from the submission deadline. 
15. Coursework submitted later than seven calendar days after the published deadline, without an 
agreed extension, will receive a mark of zero.  
 
Grace period 
16. An automatic grace period of four hours will be granted to take account of unanticipated short-term 
issues near the submission deadline, during which no penalty will be applied.  
17. When submitting coursework through Myplace, students must indicate that they wish the ‘grace 
period’ to apply and indicate the nature of the issue that has caused the late submission of the 
assignment.  
18. IT issues and travel problems would be accepted as valid grounds for late submission for up to four 
hours after the submission deadline, although alone these would not be valid grounds for lengthier 
extension requests.  
19. Penalties will be applied according to the sliding scale set out in this policy when the four-hour 
grace period ends.

Penalties for late submission  
20. Coursework submitted after the published deadline, or approved extension deadline, will be subject 
to penalties on a sliding percentage scale.  
21. Coursework that is submitted late, but within 24 hours of the submission deadline, or approved 
extension submission deadline, will be subject to a 10-point deduction on the percentage mark 
being applied to the original mark, until or unless the percentage mark reaches or is 40% for 
undergraduate coursework, or 50% for postgraduate and final year of integrated master’s 
coursework. 
22. For each subsequent day, or part day, that coursework is submitted late, up until a maximum of 
seven calendar days from the deadline, a penalty of 5 percentage point deduction per day or part 
day will be applied to the original mark until or unless the percentage mark reaches or is 40% for 
undergraduate coursework, or 50% for postgraduate and level 5 of integrated master’s coursework.  
23. For coursework submitted late (but within the seven calendar day window) that is of a pass 
standard, a minimum mark of 40% will be applied to undergraduate coursework; and a minimum 
mark of 50% will be applied to postgraduate and level 5 of integrated master's coursework.  
24. For coursework submitted late (but within the seven calendar day window) where the academic 
quality is below the pass mark, no penalty should be applied, with the emphasis on supporting the 
student in any resubmission. 
25. All coursework submitted before the date at which feedback is returned to students (this is usually 
15 working days in line with the Assessment and Feedback Policy) will be marked at face value 
with feedback provided, irrespective of the final mark recorded. That is, all students should be 
made aware of the original mark given before the application of any penalty for late submission., 
26. Coursework submitted beyond the date at which feedback is returned to students will not be 
marked. 
27. For coursework that is submitted only in hard copy to the Department, staff shall not include the 
weekend or University closure days within the calculation of penalties.  
 
Assessments that are marked on a non-numerical scale (e.g. Pass/Fail, Satisfactory/Non-
satisfactory 
28. The sliding scale penalty cannot naturally be applied to coursework marked on a non-numerical 
scale.  
29. Where coursework is marked on a non-numerical scale, and it is submitted within seven calendar 
days of the submission deadline, and is of a Pass standard, the coursework will be awarded a 
Pass.  
30. Where coursework is marked on a non-numerical scale, and it is submitted more than seven 
calendar days after the submission deadline, the coursework shall be awarded a Fail.

Extensions  
32. Students can request an extension to their coursework submission deadline when they can 
demonstrate that there are exceptional circumstances that are impacting their ability to meet the 
submission date. Students must submit their request for an extension as early as possible, and in 
line with the Policy on Extensions to the Submission of Coursework. This must normally be before 
the submission deadline. For ongoing, longer-term personal issues which are negatively impacting 
their attendance and studies in general, students should follow the guidance set out in the Personal 
Circumstances and Academic Appeals Procedure.  
 
Roles and responsibilities  
33. Departmental Staff are responsible for:  
a. Communicating this policy to students;  
b. Ensuring this policy is implemented consistently across programmes; and 
c. Reassuring students that they will not be disadvantaged if an assessment is submitted late due 
to known technical issues with Myplace. 
 
34. Students are responsible for:  
a. Understanding the requirements of individual assessments, and actively engaging with 
assessment tasks by devoting appropriate time and effort;  
b. Finding out where, how, and when assessments are to be submitted;
