Introduction and purpose 
1. The University of Strathclyde (hereafter ‘the University’) is committed to promoting the research and 
knowledge of the University to the widest possible audience through Open Research practices. This 
policy outlines the University’s support for Open Access, one of the tenets of Open Research. This policy 
promotes the aims of Strathclyde 2030, the University of Strathclyde Research Publications Guidance  
and the established institutional practice of complying with the R esearch Excellence Framework (REF) 
Open Access Policy.   
2. This policy should be read alongside other relevant University and sectoral policies and guidance listed 
in the cover sheet, and it supersedes previous University open access guidance.   
3. The purpose of this policy is to: 
i. Enable the University to disseminate its research and scholarship as widely as possible; and 
ii. Support University authors to meet external open access requirements. 
4. Questions relating to this policy should be directed to the University’s Open Access team at 
openaccess@strath.ac.uk
.  
 
Scope  
5. This policy applies to all authors or co-authors who are affiliated with the University (hereafter 'Authors'). 
Publishing activities are carried out by Authors at all levels across the University, therefore the 
requirements to make eligible publications open access includes all University staff (including honorary 
and visiting) and students who publish while affiliated with the University. This is in line with the definition 
outlined in section 1.3 of the Research Code of Practice:  
i. All University staff (regardless of contract type) engaging in research activities.  
ii. All University students engaging in research activities.   
iii. Any individuals who do not fall within (i) and (ii) above but who are otherwise associated with the 
University and are authorised to use the University’s name, facilities and/or services when 
engaging in research activities (such persons would include, for example, those holding visiting, 
honorary or emeritus status).  
6. It is the responsibility of all Strathclyde authors to ensure that they meet this policy, even if they are not 
the corresponding author.     
7. This policy applies to all articles, authored or co- authored while the individual  is a member of staff or 
student at Strathclyde. This includes conference proceedings published with an International Standard 
Serial Number (ISSN).  
8. This policy does not apply to preprints. A preprint is a version of an article that precedes formal peer view 
and publication in an academic journal.  
9. Thi s policy does not  apply to monographs, books, book chapters, edited collections or other research 
outputs although the University strongly encourages authors to make them as openly available as 
possible.   
10. This policy applies to all in scope outputs that have been submitted for publication from 1 September 
2025, the start date of this policy.   
11. Authors who are in receipt of funding from organisations such as UK Research and Innovation (UKRI), 
the Wellcome Trust, European Commission, etc., must also adhere to their funder’s open access policies 
and ensure that all outputs related to this funding complies with their stated policies.   
 
Policy requirements    
12. From 1 September 2025, all in -scope articles submitted to journals by University authors must be 
deposited in the institutional repository within 3 months of the acceptance date and made open access 
with a Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) licence (see Appendix A
 for more information on licence 
types). There are multiple routes available for Authors to comply with this Open Access Policy:

i. Green Open Access via the Institutional Rights Retention Policy    
Green open access is where the author publishes in a journal and then deposits the accepted version 
of this article into a subject or institutional repository. The University of Strathclyde has an Institutional 
Rights Retention Policy t hat allows authors to make the accepted manuscripts of in-scope outputs open 
access immediately on publication with a CC BY licence  by including specific wording in the 
acknowledgements section of the manuscript. All outputs covered by the Open Access policy will 
automatically be compliant if deposited in accordance with the University of Strathclyde’s Institutional 
Rights Retention Policy . A uthors should ensure their accepted manuscripts are deposited in the 
institutional repository via Pure within three months of acceptance.   
ii. Diamond Open Access    
Diamond open access is a publication model where journals or similar platforms do not charge fees to 
either the author or reader. Diamond Open Access journals that do not require an article processing 
charge (APC) can be found using resources such as DIAMAS  and the 
Directory of Open Access 
Journals. Authors must ensure that their outputs are published with a Creative Commons licence and 
deposited in the University’s institutional repository via Pure.     
iii. Gold Open Access via Read & Publish deals or APC payment    
Gold open access includes outputs where the final published version of the output is made open to 
everyone, often requiring the payment of a fee or Article Processing Charge (APC).  
a. The University subscribes to a number of Read & Publish deals where eligible corresponding 
authors can publish Gold open access at no additional cost to themselves. Authors must select a 
CC BY licence when using University  Read & Publish deals or when an APC has been paid. 
Authors must ensure that the published version is deposited in the University’s institutional 
repository via Pure.    
b. Authors in receipt of external research funding have the option of using the funds provided to the 
University by specific funders to make eligible publications open access. Authors should contact 
openaccess@strath.ac.uk prior to submission to ensure the output meets their 
funder’s open 
access requirements an d are eligible for APC support.     
c. Institutional Open Access Fund - The IOAF is a small fund provided by the University to meet the 
cost of publishing in fully Open Access journals where the Authors have no other funding available 
to cover these APCs. Preference for funding from the IOAF wi ll be given to Early Career 
Researchers. Further guidance can be found on the Funding Open Access webpage
, and Authors 
can contact openaccess@strath.ac.uk to check eligibility.     
13. All outputs covered by this policy must be deposited in the institutional repository via Pure . Information 
on how to do this can be found in the Pure guidance pages on Sharepoint.    
14. Where the University of Strathclyde author is not the corresponding author and/or is not able to select 
the required Creative Commons licence, they can meet the requirements of this policy by following the 
Institutional Rights Retention route noted above (12.i.).      
15. The University recognises that there may be cases, and outside of an individual’s control, where it may 
not be possible to use a CC BY licence. In these cases, it may be possible to apply an alternative Creative 
Commons licence after discussion with the Open Access team
.  
16. For all publications, any related research data must be deposited in accordance with the University’s 
Research Data Management and Sharing Policy and Authors must also include a Data Access Statement
.   
 
Further guidance and support   
17. A general overview of the University’s Open Access support and guidance can be found on the Library 
Open Access Information webpage.   
18. Authors can consult the University’s SciFree website to find journals that are included in our current Read 
& Publish deals.  
19. Questions relating to this policy should be directed to the Open Access Team by email at 
openaccess@strath.ac.uk.

Appendix A: Creative Commons Licences   
 
For information, a summary of Creative Commons Licences is given below. Authors may wish to consult the 
more comprehensive overview of available licences from Creative Commons.  
• CC 0 – the work is in the public domain and the creator(s) has given up their copyright. There are no 
conditions on how the work can be used.  
• CC BY – anyone can distribute, remix, reuse and re-share the work as long as they provide attribution 
to the original creator(s).  
• CC BY-SA –  anyone can distribute, remix, adapt/build upon the original as long as they provide 
attribution to the original creator(s) and the resulting work is licences under the same terms as the 
original.  
• CC BY-NC – anyone can distribute, remix, adapt/build upon the original, but only for non-commercial 
purposes. They must also provide attribution to the original creator(s).  
• CC BY -NC-SA – anyone can distribute, remix, adapt/build upon the original, but only for non -
commercial purposes, and the resulting work is licences under the same terms as the original. They 
must also provide attribution to the original creator(s).  
• CC BY-ND – anyone can distribute the original work, though they cannot adapt the work in any way 
and attribution must be given to the creator(s).  
• CC BY-NC-ND – anyone can distribute the original work, though they cannot adapt the work in any 
way, and it cannot be used for commercial purposes. Attribution must be given to the creator(s).
